THE CHRONICLE· PROG-NET 3.0

Prog-Net 1.0

Prog-Net 1.0 began as a learning-based experiment and gradually developed into a more structured software project. What initially started as a collection of small programs became a central idea: a connected system of multiple applications accessible through a lobby.

Documentation pageDetails subject to confirmation

#From an Experiment to Prog-Net

What initially started as a learning-based experiment gradually developed into a structured software project. As the collection of applications grew, we began looking beyond individual programs and considered how they could work together as part of a single system. We named it ProgNet, an abbreviation of Program Network, and created a lobby that connected multiple applications and presented the available choices to users.

i

The name reflects the idea of a connected collection of programs, not a computer network or internet system.

#The Applications Behind the Network

Prog-Net 1.0 brought together a collection of 19 applications divided into main and supportive categories. The main applications were accessible through the central lobby, while supportive applications worked behind the scenes to enable the system's functionality. Together, these components formed the foundation of Program Network: a central interface connecting multiple applications within one project ecosystem.

i

The project combined a central lobby with a range of smaller utility and game applications.

#Two Months of Consistent Effort

The development of Prog-Net 1.0 required more than programming skills. It demanded consistency, collaboration, creative thinking, and a willingness to keep working throughout the available timeline. For two months, the team focused on developing and expanding the project, using the full period to improve the number and quality of applications rather than treating the deadline as an end point.

i

The collective team effort was central to the project’s early growth.

#More Than Just Code

Prog-Net 1.0 taught us that software development is not limited to writing code. It involves observing problems, exploring ideas, making decisions, working consistently, and finding ways to bring different components together. One of the biggest lessons was the value of execution: an idea may look simple on paper, but turning it into a working application requires time, effort, experimentation, and problem-solving.

i

The first generation gave the team practical experience and prepared it for bigger challenges ahead.
